<h3 id="when-it-doesn-t-fit"><a class="anchor" href="#when-it-doesn-t-fit" aria-hidden="true">#</a>When it doesn&#39;t fit</h3><p>A wide video in a tall format (or the other way round) leaves empty space. <strong>When it doesn&#39;t fit</strong> decides what fills it:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Blur</strong>: a blurred, slightly darker copy of the video fills the space behind it. This is the usual look for a wide video in a vertical post. It&#39;s the default.</li>
<li><strong>Bars</strong>: black bars.</li>
<li><strong>Crop</strong>: the video is enlarged to fill the whole frame, and the edges are cut off.</li>
</ul>

<h3 id="my-styles"><a class="anchor" href="#my-styles" aria-hidden="true">#</a>My styles</h3><p>Found a look you like? Keep it for every project:</p>
<ol>
<li>Under the settings, press <strong>Save as my style</strong>.</li>
<li>Give it a name (up to 40 characters) and save it.</li>
</ol>
<p>It keeps the whole look: font, colours, outline, shadow, size, position and animation. It shows first under <strong>Preset</strong>, in <strong>My styles</strong>, in every project, and in the <strong>Subtitle style</strong> step of <a href="/kroma/docs/0.2/batch/">Batch</a>. Picking it keeps the project&#39;s own <strong>Bilingual</strong> setting.</p>
<p>When the current look is already saved, the row says so: <em>This look is saved as &quot;…&quot;</em>. To delete one, point at its tile and press its delete button. Projects that use it keep their look.</p>
<p>My styles are kept in <code>my-styles.json</code> in Kroma&#39;s settings folder.</p>
<h2 id="hide-original-subtitles"><a class="anchor" href="#hide-original-subtitles" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Hide original subtitles</h2><p>Many videos, especially Chinese dramas, have subtitles burned into the picture. Your new subtitles would sit on top of them and be hard to read. Kroma can find them and blur them for you:</p>
<ol>
<li>Open <strong>Objects</strong>.</li>
<li>Press <strong>Hide original subtitles</strong>. It says <strong>Looking for burned-in subtitles…</strong> while it works.</li>
</ol>
<p>Kroma looks at about 30 frames across the video for the strip in the lower part of the picture where text keeps coming back, and puts a blur box exactly over it, for the whole video. The box follows the picture, so it&#39;s in the right place whether the video uses <strong>Blur</strong>, <strong>Bars</strong> or <strong>Crop</strong>.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Found</strong>: <em>Covered the burned-in subtitles</em> The box is selected, so you can fine-tune it: drag it, drag its corner, or change <strong>Blur</strong> (30 to start with).</li>
<li><strong>None found</strong>: <em>No burned-in subtitles found</em> Kroma still adds a box where subtitles usually are, so you can move it, or delete it.</li>
</ul>
<p>Pressing it again replaces the box it placed before. It works on the first video on the timeline.</p>
<p>To do this for a whole series, turn on <strong>Hide original subtitles</strong> in <a href="/kroma/docs/0.2/batch/">Batch</a>. Covers made by Kroma keep the hidden subtitles blurred too.</p>
<h2 id="objects"><a class="anchor" href="#objects" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Objects</h2><p><strong>Objects</strong> lets you place things on top of the video. Your subtitles always stay above them.</p>
<p>Under <strong>Add to the video</strong>:</p>
<div class="table-wrap"><table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Button</th>
<th>What you get</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td><strong>Hide original subtitles</strong></td>
<td>Finds the burned-in subtitles and blurs them (see above)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Blur area</strong></td>
<td>A blur box in the middle of the picture, to hide a watermark, a face or a number plate</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Image</strong></td>
<td>Your logo or a sponsor&#39;s, from a <code>.png</code>, <code>.jpg</code>, <code>.jpeg</code>, <code>.webp</code> or <code>.gif</code> file. It starts in the top right corner.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Text</strong></td>
<td>A short line of text, like a call to action. It starts in the top left, saying &quot;Sponsored by …&quot;.</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table></div>
<p>The buttons are greyed out until there&#39;s a video on the timeline.</p>
<h3 id="moving-and-resizing"><a class="anchor" href="#moving-and-resizing" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Moving and resizing</h3><p>On the preview:</p>
<ul>
<li>Click an object to select it. It gets a dashed white outline.</li>
<li>Drag it to move it.</li>
<li>Drag the round handle at its bottom right corner to resize it.</li>
</ul>
<p>Images keep their shape when you resize them. For text, making the box taller makes the letters bigger; the width follows the text.</p>

<h3 id="showing-an-object-for-part-of-the-video"><a class="anchor" href="#showing-an-object-for-part-of-the-video" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Showing an object for part of the video</h3><p>Every object starts out shown for the whole video. To show it only for a while:</p>
<ol>
<li>Select the object.</li>
<li>Turn off <strong>Whole video</strong>. It now starts where the playhead is, and lasts until the end. The switch shows the times, like <em>Shown 00:10 → end</em>.</li>
<li>Move the playhead and press <strong>Start here</strong> or <strong>End here</strong> under <strong>Timing</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<h3 id="the-list-of-objects"><a class="anchor" href="#the-list-of-objects" aria-hidden="true">#</a>The list of objects</h3><p><strong>On the video</strong> lists every object you&#39;ve added. Click a name to select it. Use the eye button to <strong>Hide</strong> or <strong>Show</strong> an object (hidden ones aren&#39;t exported), and the bin to <strong>Delete</strong> it. You can also select an object and press <code>Delete</code>.</p>
