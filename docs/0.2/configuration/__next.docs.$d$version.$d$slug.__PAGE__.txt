32:T303a,<p>Kroma uses online AI services to listen to videos, translate them and write your posts. You use them with <strong>your own keys</strong>. An API key is a long password that a service gives you, so it knows the requests come from your account. You pay Kroma nothing. You only pay what a service charges, and most of them are free to start.</p>
<h2 id="the-short-version"><a class="anchor" href="#the-short-version" aria-hidden="true">#</a>The short version</h2><p>For most people, one free key is enough:</p>
<ol>
<li>Get a free <strong>Gemini</strong> key at <a href="https://aistudio.google.com/apikey" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">Google AI Studio</a>.</li>
<li>In Kroma, open <strong>Settings → Translation</strong>, choose <strong>Gemini</strong>, paste the key and click <strong>Save</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>That one key covers translation, speech-to-text, finding characters, finding names for the series glossary, and writing social posts. Everything below is optional.</p>
<h2 id="what-happens-the-first-time-you-open-kroma"><a class="anchor" href="#what-happens-the-first-time-you-open-kroma" aria-hidden="true">#</a>What happens the first time you open Kroma</h2><p>The very first time you open Kroma, it opens <strong>Settings → Translation</strong> for you. After that it opens where you work. Add a key, or choose <strong>Google Translate</strong> (no key needed), then click <strong>Back to app</strong> at the top left or press <code>Esc</code> to go to the editor.</p>
<p>You can come back any time with the <strong>Settings</strong> button at the bottom of the left rail, or <code>⌘ ,</code> on a Mac (<code>Ctrl+,</code> on Windows and Linux).</p>

<h3 id="gemini"><a class="anchor" href="#gemini" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Gemini</h3><ul>
<li><strong>API key</strong>: click <strong>Get a key</strong> to open Google AI Studio. No card is needed.</li>
<li><strong>Model</strong>: Kroma lists the models your key can use, and new versions appear by themselves. The default is <code>gemini-flash-latest</code>, which Google keeps pointed at its current Flash model. Flash models are fast and free. Pro is better but has a smaller free limit.</li>
</ul>
<p>The free tier limits how many requests you can send per minute. When that happens, Kroma waits and tries again. If Gemini stays busy, or your free daily limit runs out, Kroma switches to the lighter Flash-Lite model. Lines Gemini still couldn&#39;t translate are done by Google Translate, and Kroma tells you how many.</p>
<p>Google may use free-tier requests to improve its models. Keep that in mind for private videos.</p>
<h3 id="claude"><a class="anchor" href="#claude" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Claude</h3><ul>
<li><strong>API key</strong>: starts with <code>sk-ant-</code>. Click <strong>Get a key</strong> to open the Claude Console. You pay Anthropic for what you use.</li>
<li><strong>Model</strong>: <strong>Opus 5.5 · best Khmer</strong> (the default), <strong>Sonnet 5.5 · cheaper</strong>, or <strong>Haiku 5.5 · cheapest</strong>.</li>
</ul>
<p>If you choose Gemini but have only a Claude key, Kroma uses Claude for characters and posts.</p>
<h3 id="speed"><a class="anchor" href="#speed" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Speed</h3><p><strong>Parallel requests</strong> sets how many batches run at once for translation and speech-to-text: <strong>2</strong>, <strong>4</strong> (the default), <strong>6</strong> or <strong>8</strong>. Higher is faster. Lower it if a service says you&#39;re sending too much.</p>
<h2 id="speech-to-text"><a class="anchor" href="#speech-to-text" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Speech to Text</h2><p>Speech-to-text turns the video&#39;s speech into timed lines. Kroma only needs it when the video didn&#39;t come with the site&#39;s own subtitles. Open <strong>Settings → Speech to Text</strong> and choose a service:</p>
<div class="table-wrap"><table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Service</th>
<th>What the app says</th>
<th>Notes</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td><strong>Gemini</strong></td>
<td>Your Gemini key · accurate</td>
<td>The default. Uses the key from Settings → Translation, so there&#39;s nothing else to set up. Very accurate, with punctuation, but takes about a minute for every 2 minutes of speech.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Groq</strong></td>

<p>For long movies, Groq is much faster than Gemini.</p>
<p>With Groq or OpenAI, an <strong>Advanced</strong> card shows the <strong>Endpoint</strong> and <strong>Model</strong>. You can point Kroma at any OpenAI-compatible transcription service there. Most people never need to change these.</p>
<h2 id="voice-engine-optional"><a class="anchor" href="#voice-engine-optional" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Voice Engine (optional)</h2><p>The instant voices from Microsoft need no key. For cloned voices (made from a short recording) and designed voices (storyteller, news anchor and so on), Kroma uses its own voice engine, built into the app. In <strong>Settings → Voice Engine</strong>, press <strong>Install voice engine</strong> once (about 2 GB), then <strong>Start engine</strong>. To use a free Google Colab GPU instead, switch <strong>Where it runs</strong> to <strong>Google Colab</strong> and enter its <strong>Engine address</strong> and <strong>Token</strong>. The status row shows whether the engine is on. See <a href="/kroma/docs/0.2/voice-engine/">Voice engine</a>.</p>
<h2 id="posting-optional"><a class="anchor" href="#posting-optional" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Posting (optional)</h2><p>To post to YouTube, Facebook and TikTok in one click, add an <strong>Upload-Post</strong> key in <strong>Settings → Posting</strong>, then press <strong>Connect</strong> to sign in to each platform. YouTube and Facebook work on Upload-Post&#39;s free plan (10 posts a month). TikTok needs their Basic plan. See <a href="/kroma/docs/0.2/posting/">Posting</a>.</p>
<h2 id="downloads-and-folders"><a class="anchor" href="#downloads-and-folders" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Downloads and folders</h2><p>Not keys, but worth setting once:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Settings → Downloads → Use my logins from</strong>: some sites, like Bilibili, only give 1080p, or only play some videos, when you&#39;re signed in. Choose the browser you&#39;re signed in with: Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge or Brave. Your system may ask to allow access. Safari also needs Full Disk Access. See <a href="/kroma/docs/0.2/downloading/">Downloading</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Settings → Folders</strong>: where exports and downloads go. By default it&#39;s your Movies folder (Mac) or Videos folder (Windows, Linux), then <strong>Kroma Studio → Exports</strong> and <strong>Kroma Studio → downloads</strong>. See <a href="/kroma/docs/0.2/settings/">Settings</a>.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="saving-replacing-and-removing-a-key"><a class="anchor" href="#saving-replacing-and-removing-a-key" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Saving, replacing and removing a key</h2><p>Every key field works the same way:</p>
<ol>
<li>Paste the key and click <strong>Save</strong>. A green <strong>Saved</strong> badge appears next to the label.</li>
<li>Kroma never shows a saved key again, not even to you. To change it, click <strong>Replace</strong>, paste the new one and click <strong>Save</strong> (or <strong>Cancel</strong>).</li>
<li>To delete it, click <strong>Remove</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>Settings save as soon as you change them. There&#39;s no separate Save button for the page.</p>
<h2 id="how-your-keys-are-kept-safe"><a class="anchor" href="#how-your-keys-are-kept-safe" aria-hidden="true">#</a>How your keys are kept safe</h2><ul>
<li>Keys are stored in Kroma&#39;s <code>settings.json</code> file, encrypted with your system&#39;s keychain (the macOS Keychain, Windows&#39; data protection, or the Linux keyring). On a Linux computer with no keyring available, they&#39;re stored without encryption.</li>
<li>Each key is only sent to its own service. Your Gemini key only goes to Google, your Groq key only to Groq, and so on.</li>
<li>Kroma has no server of its own. Nothing goes through Kroma.</li>
<li>Keys stay saved through updates. If a new version of Kroma can&#39;t read a key from the Mac&#39;s keychain, the key is kept, and <strong>Settings</strong> says <em>A key is saved, but this version of Kroma can&#39;t read it from the keychain</em>. Paste it again, or choose <strong>Always Allow</strong> when your Mac asks.</li>
</ul>
