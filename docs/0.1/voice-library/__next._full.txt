2b:T21b0,<p>The voice library is where you listen to every voice Kroma has, side by side, and pick one for your project. Open it with <strong>Voices</strong> in the left menu (below the editor&#39;s tools). The page is called <strong>My voices</strong>.</p>
<p>At the top right there is a button:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Clone voice</strong> makes a new voice from a short recording. See <a href="/kroma/docs/0.1/voice-cloning/">Voice cloning</a>. It&#39;s greyed out when the <a href="/kroma/docs/0.1/voice-engine/">voice engine</a> isn&#39;t connected.</li>
</ul>
<p>The page has three sections: <strong>Your voices</strong>, <strong>Preset voices</strong> and <strong>Instant voices</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="voice-cards"><a class="anchor" href="#voice-cards" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Voice cards</h2><p>Every voice is a card. A card shows:</p>
<ul>
<li>the voice&#39;s name,</li>
<li>a short line under it: the sample&#39;s length (for example &quot;12.4 s&quot;), &quot;cloned&quot;, &quot;instant · Khmer&quot; or <strong>no sample yet</strong>,</li>
<li>a green <strong>Ready</strong> badge when the voice has a sample you can play,</li>
<li>a waveform player (see below),</li>
<li>the text the sample says, or a description of the voice.</li>
</ul>
<p>The <strong>⋯</strong> button on a card opens a menu:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Use in this project</strong> makes this the project&#39;s voice (the default voice in the <strong>Voice</strong> panel) and turns the voice-over on. A message confirms it, for example: &quot;Narrator (deep male)&quot; is the voice for this project.</li>
<li><strong>Delete</strong> (for your voices) or <strong>Delete the sample</strong> (for preset voices). Deleting a cloned voice asks you to confirm first.</li>
</ul>
<p>To give a voice to one character instead, use the voice menu next to that character in the <strong>Voice</strong> panel. See <a href="/kroma/docs/0.1/characters-and-voices/">Characters &amp; voices</a>.</p>
<h2 id="the-waveform-player"><a class="anchor" href="#the-waveform-player" aria-hidden="true">#</a>The waveform player</h2><p>Each card has a small player:</p>
<ul>
<li>Press the round ▶ button to play. Press it again to pause.</li>
<li>The bars show the shape of the sound. They fill in as it plays.</li>
<li>Click anywhere on the bars to jump to that point.</li>
<li>The times under the bars show where you are and how long the sample is.</li>
</ul>
<p>Only one sample plays at a time. Starting one stops any other.</p>
<h2 id="your-voices"><a class="anchor" href="#your-voices" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Your voices</h2><p>Voices you cloned. The section reminds you: &quot;Only use voices you have permission to use.&quot;</p>
<p>Each card shows the length of the recording and the words it says. If you have none yet, it says &quot;No cloned voices yet.&quot;</p>
<p>Cloned voices need the <a href="/kroma/docs/0.1/voice-engine/">voice engine</a> to speak.</p>

<h3 id="making-a-sample"><a class="anchor" href="#making-a-sample" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Making a sample</h3><p>A preset voice is designed from a written description, for example &quot;A middle-aged man with a deep, clear, confident male narrator voice&quot;. The first time, the engine makes a sample of about 10 seconds from that description. That sample is then saved on your computer and used every time, so the voice <strong>sounds the same in every video</strong>.</p>
<p>On a card with <strong>no sample yet</strong>, press <strong>Make a sample (once)</strong>. It takes about a minute. The button says <strong>Making the sample…</strong> while it works. Kroma checks that the result has the right pitch for the character (a man sounds like a man, a child like a child), and tries again if not.</p>
<p>If the engine isn&#39;t connected, the button says <strong>Start the engine to make a sample</strong>.</p>
<p>You don&#39;t have to make samples by hand. If you use a preset voice in a voice-over before it has a sample, Kroma makes the sample first (&quot;Preparing the voice … (once)&quot;).</p>
<p><strong>Delete the sample</strong> removes the saved sample. The next time you use the voice, a new one is made, and it may sound a little different.</p>
