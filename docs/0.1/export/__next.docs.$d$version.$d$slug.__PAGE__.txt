35:T1fed,<p>Exporting turns your project into one video file, with the subtitles, objects and voice-over burned in. The file works everywhere: YouTube, Facebook, TikTok, your phone.</p>
<h2 id="start-an-export"><a class="anchor" href="#start-an-export" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Start an export</h2><p>There are three ways, and they all open the same menu of sizes:</p>
<ul>
<li>The <strong>Export</strong> button at the top right. It shows your format, like <strong>Export 16:9</strong>.</li>
<li>The <strong>Export</strong> step in the step bar at the bottom.</li>
<li><strong>Next: Export the video</strong>, on the right of the step bar, once the earlier steps are done.</li>
</ul>
<p>They&#39;re greyed out until there&#39;s a video on the timeline.</p>
<h2 id="sizes"><a class="anchor" href="#sizes" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Sizes</h2><p>Pick one of three sizes. Each one shows how big the file will be for your video, like <em>about 75 MB</em>.</p>
<div class="table-wrap"><table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Size</th>
<th>What the menu says</th>
<th>How it&#39;s made</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td><strong>Small</strong></td>
<td>For TikTok and Facebook: they shrink videos anyway.</td>
<td>Squeezed to a smaller file (about 2 Mbit/s at 1080p)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Standard</strong></td>
<td>Fastest: uses the Mac&#39;s video chip. Good for YouTube.</td>
<td>On a Mac, made by the Mac&#39;s built-in video chip (about 4 Mbit/s at 1080p)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>High</strong></td>
<td>Best quality, about 3× slower to export.</td>
<td>Close to the original quality, with better sound (192 kbit/s instead of 128)</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table></div>
<p>On Windows and Linux, <strong>Standard</strong> says <em>Quick to export. Good for YouTube.</em> and uses a fast setting on the processor instead of the video chip.</p>
<h3 id="how-big-will-it-be"><a class="anchor" href="#how-big-will-it-be" aria-hidden="true">#</a>How big will it be?</h3><p>Rough sizes for a 5-minute video, from the estimate Kroma shows:</p>

<p>These are estimates. <strong>High</strong> changes the most from video to video: a calm, simple picture can come out smaller than <strong>Standard</strong>, a busy one bigger. A video over 1,000 MB shows in GB, like <em>about 1.2 GB</em>.</p>
<h3 id="which-one-to-choose"><a class="anchor" href="#which-one-to-choose" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Which one to choose</h3><ul>
<li>Posting to <strong>TikTok</strong> or <strong>Facebook</strong>? Choose <strong>Small</strong>. They compress every upload again anyway, so a bigger file doesn&#39;t look better there, it just takes longer to upload.</li>
<li>Posting to <strong>YouTube</strong>? <strong>Standard</strong> is quick and looks good.</li>
<li>Keeping a master copy, or the picture has lots of fine detail? Choose <strong>High</strong>, and give it more time.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="what-you-get"><a class="anchor" href="#what-you-get" aria-hidden="true">#</a>What you get</h2><p>Every export is an <strong>MP4</strong> video (H.264 picture, AAC sound) at 30 frames a second, the format every platform accepts. Its size in pixels comes from the format you chose in <strong>Style</strong>:</p>

</tr>

<td>9:16</td>
<td>1080 × 1920</td>
</tr>

<td>1:1</td>
<td>1080 × 1080</td>

<p>The file is set up to start playing before it has fully loaded, which helps when it&#39;s played online.</p>
<h2 id="following-the-export"><a class="anchor" href="#following-the-export" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Following the export</h2><p>While it works, a progress bar shows at the top right, next to the buttons:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Drawing subtitles 25/312</strong>: Kroma draws each subtitle as a picture, so the export looks exactly like the preview.</li>
<li>If there&#39;s a voice-over, Kroma puts the voice track together.</li>
<li><strong>Exporting · 1:23 / 5:00</strong>: the video itself, with how far it has got.</li>
</ol>
<p>The <strong>Export</strong> button becomes <strong>Cancel export</strong> while it runs. Press it to stop.</p>
<p>When it&#39;s done, Kroma says <strong>Export finished</strong> with the file&#39;s location, and opens the folder with the new video selected.</p>
<h2 id="where-the-file-is-saved"><a class="anchor" href="#where-the-file-is-saved" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Where the file is saved</h2><p>Exports go straight into your export folder. By default that&#39;s:</p>
<ul>
<li>Mac: <code>Movies/Kroma Studio/Exports</code></li>
<li>Windows: <code>Videos/Kroma Studio/Exports</code></li>
</ul>
<p>The file is named after the project and the format, like <code>My drama (9x16).mp4</code>. Kroma never overwrites a file: if that name is taken, it saves <code>My drama (9x16) (2).mp4</code>, and so on.</p>
<p>To change this, open <strong>Settings → Folders</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Export folder</strong>: press <strong>Change…</strong> to pick another folder, <strong>Reset</strong> to go back to the default, or <strong>Open</strong> to see it.</li>
<li><strong>Ask where to save each time</strong>: turn it on to choose the name and place for every export. Off (the default), Kroma saves straight into the folder.</li>
</ul>
<p>Subtitle <code>.srt</code> files saved from <strong>Post</strong> go to the same folder. See <a href="/kroma/docs/0.1/posting/">Posting</a>.</p>
<h2 id="with-a-voice-over"><a class="anchor" href="#with-a-voice-over" aria-hidden="true">#</a>With a voice-over</h2><p>If you made a voice-over (see <a href="/kroma/docs/0.1/characters-and-voices/">Characters &amp; voices</a>), it&#39;s mixed into the export, as long as <strong>Use the voice-over</strong> is on in <strong>Voice → Mix</strong>.</p>
<h3 id="removing-the-original-voices"><a class="anchor" href="#removing-the-original-voices" aria-hidden="true">#</a>Removing the original voices</h3><p>A voice-over sounds best without the original speech underneath. <strong>Remove the original voices</strong> (in <strong>Voice → Mix</strong>) keeps the music and sound effects and drops the original speech. It&#39;s on unless you turn it off.</p>
<p>Usually Kroma does this right after the voice-over is made. If it hasn&#39;t happened yet, Kroma does it when you press export, before the export starts. That takes about 40 seconds per 5 minutes of video on a recent Mac or PC, and it&#39;s instant afterwards.</p>
<p>How loud things are in the export:</p>
<ul>
<li>With the voices removed, the background plays at the <strong>Music &amp; effects</strong> level (100% by default).</li>
<li>With <strong>Remove the original voices</strong> off, the original sound plays at the <strong>Original sound</strong> level (15% by default) under the voice-over.</li>
</ul>
<p>If removing the voices fails, Kroma tells you <em>&quot;The original voices are still in&quot;</em>, and the original sound plays quietly under the voice-over. Try again from <strong>Voice → Mix</strong>.</p>
<p>If some lines don&#39;t have a voice yet, Kroma warns you: <em>&quot;Some lines have no voice yet; they&#39;ll be silent. Press &#39;Make voice-over&#39; to fill them.&quot;</em></p>
<h2 id="when-an-export-fails"><a class="anchor" href="#when-an-export-fails" aria-hidden="true">#</a>When an export fails</h2><p>If the export stops with an error, Kroma saves the full details as <code>last-export-error.log</code> in its settings folder (on a Mac: <code>~/Library/Application Support/Kroma Studio</code>). Attach that file to a <a href="https://github.com/Heang-Dev/KROMA.STUDIO/issues" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">GitHub issue</a> so it can be fixed. See <a href="/kroma/docs/0.1/troubleshooting/">Troubleshooting</a>.</p>
<p>If the top of the window says <strong>Missing ffmpeg</strong>, the tool that makes the video isn&#39;t installed. It shows the command to install it.</p>
